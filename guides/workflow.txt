# Project management — Nimblium

How we work: sources, organization, checks, tracking.

## Extracting the source text

`pdftotext -f <start> -l <end> -layout sources/Heroes.pdf -`

- The page numbers printed in the footer are offset by -1 from the PDF page requested (e.g.
  asking for PDF pages 14-17 gives footers 13-16).
- Many pages are laid out in two columns: the text can interleave between columns in the
  `pdftotext` output. If the order looks wrong, extract a single page again.
- Table of contents to find the classes: Berserker p.6, Cheat p.12, Commander p.18, Hunter
  p.24, Mage p.30, Oathsworn p.36, Shadowmancer p.42, Shepherd p.48, Songweaver p.54,
  Stormshifter p.60, Zephyr p.66, Extra Subclasses p.73.

## File organization per class (`game/class/<class_name>/`)

- The class's base abilities: `.typ` files directly in `game/class/<name>/`.
- A pool of abilities to choose from (the Cheat's Underhanded Abilities, the Hunter's Thrill
  of the Hunt, the Shepherd's Sacred Graces) goes in its own subfolder
  `game/class/<name>/<pool>/` with its own `mod.typ`, imported with
  `#import "<pool>/mod.typ": *` from the class's mod.typ.
- Subclasses go in `game/class/<name>/subclasses/<subclass>/`, each with its `mod.typ`;
  `game/class/<name>/subclasses/mod.typ` gathers every subclass; the class's mod.typ imports
  `subclasses/mod.typ`.
- Every new file must be added to the `mod.typ` of its folder (which cascades up to
  `game/class/mod.typ`). It is needed to compile and test through `#symbol_name`, even though
  `tools/export_cards.py` finds the files on its own by walking the file system.
- File names in snake_case, derived from the ability's name, punctuation removed (e.g.
  "How'd YOU get here?!" → `how_you_get_here.typ`).

## Class sheet (`game/class/<name>/<name>_class_sheet.typ`)

One A4 page per class, made with `templates/class_sheet.typ`: starting information on one line (key
stats, Vitality, armor; not the weapons (no weapon proficiency) nor the starting equipment), the table of levels 1 to 20
(what you gain at each level, max spell tier) and, at the bottom, the subclasses by level and
the lists of options. It replaces cards that would only list choices by level. No repetition:
a rule already on a card is not repeated, the sheet names the card (`#card_ref`). Sheets
done: Berserker, Cheat, Commander, Hunter, Mage, Oathsworn, Shadowmancer, Shepherd,
Songweaver, Stormshifter. Every new
class gets its sheet together with its cards: a class is not COMPLETE without it.

## Rules pages (`game/rules/<area>.typ`)

The game rules that are not on cards, made with `templates/rules_page.typ`: one A4 sheet per
area, in two columns. The recto is usually enough; when the text overflows, `#verso` opens
the verso (recto-verso, never more). The recto holds what is used at the table, the verso
what is looked up (rests, conditions...). A section (`#section("Title")[...]`) never splits
across two columns.

Planned areas:
- **Adventure** (done, recto-verso, the text flows with no forced verso): rounding, stats, skills, checks, advantage, size,
  HP, temporary HP, Rest (no Hit Dice, no Field Rest), items, making a hero (class,
  ancestry and background, stat arrays, skills, the rest of the sheet) and leveling up. No
  lodging and no downtime. Ancestries and backgrounds are cards.
- **Combat** (done): turn, pool of 3 actions, attacks, Defense, movement and reach, Cover,
  grapples in progress, Knockback, Concentration, Dying and Wounds; on the verso the
  conditions (taken from `game/conditions.typ`) and the creature rules
  (`creature_rules.txt`). The actions themselves are on the cards of `game/actions/` (Basic
  Actions, Grapple, Triggered Actions): the page refers to them without repeating them.
- **Magic** (done): schools, casting, mana, upcasting, cantrips, how to read a spell card,
  utility spells. One page. Spell scrolls merge into the Wand card (game/items/gear/).
- No equipment page: equipment will become cards.

Writing:
- The same language as the cards (`writing_rules.txt`): English, complete sentences, no
  colons or parentheses, terms through `game/terms.typ`, `check_terms.py` checks them.
- A named rule is written `#rule[Name][Text.]` (the name in bold, followed by a period). A
  keyword already defined is reused as is from `game/keywords.typ` or `game/conditions.typ`
  (`#keywords.advantage`) instead of being rewritten.
- Tables (`#values`) are reference lists: fragments are allowed in them.
- No repetition of a card: name it with `#card_ref`. No optional rules from the book, unless
  the user decides otherwise.
- A notes block at the bottom of the file, as on a card (`review:`, `house-rule:`...), with
  the source pages.

## Things that NEVER become a card

- Pure stat increases ("+1 DEX or WIL", "Key/Secondary Stat Increase").
- "Epic Boon. Choose an Epic Boon..." (a placeholder that refers to another book).
- "Subclass. Gain your X subclass feature." (a level placeholder, not an ability in itself).
- Any ability that only unlocks spells (see the rule below).

## Important rule: no card for "you just unlock a spell"

NEVER create an ability or mechanic card whose only content is "you learn/unlock this or that
spell/cantrip". In actual play, the matching spell cards are handed straight to the player: an
ability card that only says "you now know these spells" is redundant and useless. Examples
removed for this reason: `keeper_of_life_and_death.typ` ("You know Radiant and Necrotic
cantrips") and `master_of_twilight.typ` ("Choose Necrotic/Radiant Utility Spells... know all
at level 11"). The Mana pool, however, was kept as a Mechanic card because it also defines
the resource's formula and rule, not only "you get spells".

## Checks after creating or editing

Compile with a throwaway wrapper file (never committed) that imports the relevant mod.typ and
references the new symbols:
`typst compile --root <repo_root> file.typ output.pdf`
then delete the throwaway file. Also run `python3 tools/check_terms.py`. For a visual check,
export to PNG at 300ppi with a 131mm×88mm page and 0 margin (matching the actual wrapper of
`tools/export_cards.py`) and look at the image.

**Don't export PNGs every time**: only when asked, or for an occasional layout check when the
structure is really new (a new field, a new card type). If the user says "I'll handle it" for
the exports, don't make any more until asked again.

## Export

`python3 tools/export_cards.py` exports each card to PDF next to its `.typ`. Run it (with
`--book`) after every change to a card, class sheet or rules page: the user reads the PDFs,
not the sources. With `--book`,
it also creates `build/nimblium_cards.pdf`: every card, one per page, where the names written
with `#card_ref` are clickable links to the card they name (in the individual PDFs, they are
only in italics).

## Review tracking and card notes (`tools/review.py`)

Each card ends with a Typst block comment that holds its management notes. It is not card
text: `check_terms.py` ignores it.

```
/*
review: style=2026-11-14 changelog=2026-10-02

house-rule: The Spirit Companion has an HP pool of Tier × 10 instead of healing charges.

interpretation: The changelog lists the four results without numbers. Results 1 to 4
follow its order.
*/
```

- `review:`: the card was rewritten following `writing_rules.txt` of that date AND aligned
  with the official changelog of that date. Add or update it only when both are done.
  `python3 tools/review.py` lists, by class or school, the cards that are up to date and the
  ones to review (`-v` to list everything). When `writing_rules.txt` changes notably or a new
  changelog comes out, update `STYLE` / `CHANGELOG` at the top of the script: every older
  card goes back to "to review".
- `house-rule:`: we disagree with the game and deliberately change the rule. Never "fix" it
  from the book or the changelog. Also add a line to `house_rules.txt`.
- `interpretation:`: the source is unclear or incomplete, and we made a choice. To revisit if
  a clearer source comes out.
- `changelog:`: only when the card replaces or removes something from the book (a replaced
  spell, a changelog change not applied yet…). Not needed for a simple change of value.
- One note per paragraph, separated by a blank line, in English like the cards.
  `python3 tools/review.py --rules` lists them all, by tag.
- A rule that concerns a whole family goes in the folder's `mod.typ` (e.g.
  `game/creatures/goblin/mod.typ`); a rule about a keyword, in `game/keywords.typ` or
  `game/conditions.typ`. A rule that affects the whole game goes in `house_rules.txt`.

## Pace of collaboration

The user sets the pace explicitly: sometimes "the next two", sometimes "all the rest at once",
sometimes a single named item. By default, follow the order of the PDF's levels and pages,
and skip the entries that don't become cards (see the section above). When "the next two" is
ambiguous (e.g. after several levels with no card content), make a reasonable choice and move
on rather than blocking on a question: the user reviews and corrects afterwards rather than
approving beforehand.

**The user often edits files right after they are created** (rewording, fixing
kind/cost/limitation, new keyword files, simplification). These changes arrive as
system-reminders ("modified by the user or a linter"). Never undo them: they become the new
ground truth. Always read the current content of a file before editing it again (don't rely
on an outdated version from memory).
